Program Spotlight — LIFT Mentor Match: Technology-Enabled Employee Mentorship

West Valley-Mission CCD (Mission College) is Region 4 in an unprecedented 10-college, 10-region alliance formed by the Leadership Institute for Tomorrow (LIFT). Using the Mentor & Match – Professional Platform, the pilot launches a formal, systemwide mentorship program that matches employees — administrators, faculty, and especially classified professionals — by shared goals, lived experience, and career aspirations, expanding equitable access to professional development and inclusive leadership across the California Community Colleges.

GOAL 1Equitable Mentorship Access

Expand equitable access to professional development through mentorship across all employee classifications — classified professionals, part-time and full-time faculty, and administrators — at the 10-college, 10-region alliance institutions.

Activities

  • Launch the Mentor & Match – Professional Platform with alliance-wide planning, coordination, and a Summer 2026 soft launch with leadership teams.
  • Develop mentor and mentee training curriculum, marketing materials, and schedules; run Year 1 and Year 2 program launches with info sessions.
  • The campus lead informs all employees through faculty and classified senates, manager and staff meetings, and HR onboarding so everyone can participate.
  • Introduce “Culture of Care” Awards in Year 2 to honor outstanding mentorship and inclusive leadership.

How the goals connect
Application Narrative · Section 1.3

Together, these three goals form a cohesive, research-supported mentorship model that embeds equity across West Valley-Mission CCD. The Mentor & Match platform expands access to structured mentoring for every classification; a systemwide culture of belonging makes participants both mentors and mentees; and a data-driven, scalable model turns results into continuous improvement. The need is clear at Mission College: over Fall 2022–Fall 2024, classified professionals grew 7%, yet faculty received far more professional learning — 114 faculty completed the Equity Pedagogy Community of Praxis (Fall 2021–Spring 2025) versus just 19 classified professionals in the Classified Equity Certification Program. LIFT Mentor Match closes that gap for California’s future community college leaders.

Three Project Goals
From the application workplan, Section 2.1
GOAL 1 Equitable Mentorship Access
Expand access to mentorship for all employees

Make structured, technology-matched mentoring a right for every classification — classified professionals, faculty, and administrators — across the 10-college, 10-region alliance, reaching 50% of all employees by June 2028.

GOAL 2 Inclusive Leadership Culture
Cultivate a systemwide culture of belonging

Foster a mentoring ecosystem where every employee can contribute and grow — with at least 60% serving as both mentor and mentee — through meet-ups, Culture of Care Awards, and a Mentorship Reflection Journal.

GOAL 3 Data-Driven, Scalable Model
Build a measurable, equity-driven model

Use platform analytics, surveys, and a two-year impact study to advance EEO objectives — producing semester dashboards, a final Impact Report to the Chancellor’s Office, and a model for statewide replication.

Workplan Activities
By project goal · from Section 2.2
GOAL 1 · Launch
  • Plan & coordinate the alliance. System-wide collaboration, regular meetings, and shared best practices across the 10 colleges.
  • Soft launch Summer 2026. Pilot the platform with leadership teams and troubleshoot before the full rollout.
  • Develop training & marketing. Mentor and mentee curriculum, schedules, and outreach to all employee groups.
  • Year 1 & Year 2 launches. Info sessions, training, and a Year 2 relaunch with Culture of Care Awards.
GOAL 2 · Belonging
  • Host meet-ups. Two annual statewide virtual meet-ups and three regional in-person meet-ups over two years.
  • Encourage dual roles. At least 60% of participants serve as both mentor and mentee.
  • Culture of Care Awards. Recognize colleges and individuals for inclusive, equitable mentorship.
  • Reflection Journal. Publish a voluntary digital journal to share impact and transformation stories.
GOAL 3 · Data
  • Collect & analyze data. Semester dashboards on match outcomes, participation, and demographic inclusion.
  • Track and follow up. Real-time reporting with 100% follow-up on flagged concerns within 7 business days.
  • Surveys & focus groups. User-Experience Surveys in Spring 2027 and Spring 2028.
  • Impact study & summit. Two-year Program Impact Study, Impact Showcase, and Expansion Plan.
Institutional Resources Used
  • Campus Lead & Classified Senate. Amanda Marshall (EOPS Assistant Director, Classified Senate President) coordinates locally and presents to all governance groups.
  • Office of Student Equity & Success. Dean Ken Songco serves as administrator in charge, providing resources and oversight.
  • Executive Sponsor. Dr. Seher Awan, President of Mission College, champions the program and models participation with the Executive Council.
  • WVMCCD Human Resources. Distributes training and flyers, integrates the program into onboarding, and maintains visibility.
  • Leadership Institute for Tomorrow (LIFT). External coordination partner delivering the platform, training, tracking, and the impact study.
Mentor & Match Platform Features

The Mentor & Match – Professional Platform powers a cross-campus, cross-classification mentoring model with:

  • Algorithmic matching by role, goals, lived experience, and aspirations
  • Automated scheduling, direct messaging, and push notifications
  • Mentor and mentee training modules and onboarding resources
  • Real-time analytics and semester dashboards for HR and IE offices
  • Culture of Care Awards and a Mentorship Reflection Journal
